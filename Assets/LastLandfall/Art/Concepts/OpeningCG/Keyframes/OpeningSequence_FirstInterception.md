# Opening CG: First Interception

This is the revised opening sequence for the first five 8-second clips (00:00–00:40). It follows the requested order: the observatory detects an anomaly, the asteroid stream is revealed, people react to an alert, the first small wave is diverted, and the team celebrates.

## Shared visual direction

- Keep the heroine's identity consistent: short black hair, faded rust-orange weatherproof jacket, dark red life vest, plain white triangular patch on the left sleeve.
- Use cinematic painted 2.5D concept art, cool blue-gray and deep teal, restrained orange accents, matte surfaces and subtle paper grain.
- Keep faces and bodies natural. Avoid readable text, subtitles, logos, UI clutter, duplicated people, fused bodies and malformed limbs.
- The initial observatory shots stay indoors. Show outer space only after the camera reaches the monitor in the approach shot.
- The early interception is a controlled remote deflection. No piloted ship rams an asteroid, no giant explosion, and Earth remains intact.

## Flow clip prompts

### 00:00–00:08 — Observatory detects the anomaly

Use `01_Observatory_Start.png` as the exact opening frame and `02_Observatory_Detection.png` as the exact ending frame. Make one slow, steady push through a clearly recognizable mountain observatory interior: dome ribs, optical telescope and mount, work desk and night-shift lighting. The heroine notices an unusual curved cluster of rock-and-ice points on the tracking monitor; her expression shifts from concentration to focused concern. Keep the move inside the room. The dome slit shows only cloudy night sky; do not show outer space outside the building. End on the monitor and heroine. No cuts, camera shake, object morphing or readable screen text.

### 00:08–00:16 — Reveal the asteroid stream approaching Earth

Use `02_Observatory_Detection.png` as the opening reference and `03_Asteroid_Belt_Approach.png` as the ending reference. Begin on the same monitor image, slowly push into the screen, and reveal the real orbital view only after crossing the display. Show a sparse but unmistakable stream of icy fragments moving toward Earth's curved blue limb, with the larger irregular body far in the distance. Earth remains intact; no impact yet. Preserve scale, direction, cool palette and smooth camera motion. No sudden cut, no spacecraft, no fire or text.

### 00:16–00:24 — People face the alert

Use `07_Civilian_Alert_Start.png` as the opening frame and `04_Civilian_Alert.png` as the ending frame. Stay inside the same public transit hall. The warning becomes clear on the orbital display; the heroine looks up, people stop, exchange worried looks, and a parent pulls a child close. Build tension through restrained expressions and small reactions, not screaming or a stampede. Keep the heroine's face and outfit stable, the crowd anatomically clean, and the display free of readable words. One continuous, gently advancing camera move.

### 00:24–00:32 — First fragments are diverted

Use `03_Asteroid_Belt_Approach.png` as the opening frame and `05_Interception_Success.png` as the ending frame. Show only a few small incoming fragments, not the later disaster. A remote planetary-defense pulse glints beside the lead fragment and gently changes the stream's path; fragments visibly bend away from Earth and drift into empty space. Earth stays whole and calm. No piloted spacecraft, no vehicle touching an asteroid, no ramming, missiles, laser beams, fireball or huge explosion. Keep the camera steady and the deflection readable.

### 00:32–00:40 — The team celebrates

Use `08_Observatory_Tense_Start.png` as the opening frame and `06_Observatory_Celebration.png` as the ending frame. Remain inside the same observatory. The orbital trace on the monitor confirms the fragments have turned away; the heroine and team release their tension, exchange a brief hug, smile and applaud softly. Make the relief human and restrained, not a party. Keep character identities, outfits, room layout and telescope consistent. No readable text, confetti, flags or sudden camera movement.

## Flow generation status

All eight keyframes are saved in this folder and uploaded to the Flow project. Five 8-second clips were generated and placed at the start of the existing scene in the order above: observatory detection, asteroid approach, public alert, remote deflection, and observatory celebration. The following original clip transitions from a shortwave rescue room to the rescue boat, so the new opening leads into the existing rescue story. The scene now contains 32 clips and runs 04:16.

Generation used `Veo 3.1 - Fast` at x1, one generation per clip, with no rerolls; Flow charged 20 points per clip (100 points total). The Flow interface offered `Fast`, not an option named `Flash`. The scene editor's separate prompt bar still displays `Omni 1.1 Flash`; that is not the model used for these generated clips.
